Picture a giant sixty meters tall. Cut off one of its arms, and a single kick sends it bouncing across the mud like an empty cardboard box. It is lighter than styrofoam.

That is not a plot hole. It is the only way the creature survives physics.

Galileo worked this out in 1638. Scale a body up ten times and its bone cross-section grows a hundredfold — but its weight grows a thousandfold. Take human proportions to sixty meters and the femur shatters on the first step. Heat behaves the same way: it is produced by volume and shed only through surface area, so a body that large cooks itself before it can walk.

Which leaves exactly one answer — be light.
